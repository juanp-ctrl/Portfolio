Today I got my **HTB Certified Web Exploitation Specialist (CWES)** certification, and I passed the exam with **100/100 points**. It took a full year of study and a 5-day exam that pushed me more than I expected. I want to write down how it went while it's still fresh, and share what helped me in case you're preparing for it.

## The road there

I spent most of this year working through the CWES path and playing the machines of every Hack The Box season. Looking back, the habit that mattered most was simple: **I took notes on every single module.** Twenty modules, twenty sets of notes. At the time it felt slow. During the exam, those notes were what saved me.

## The exam

The thing I liked most about CWES is that it's fair. Everything you need is in the content of those 20 modules. The exam doesn't try to trick you with impossible solutions or obscure techniques you've never seen. That doesn't make it easy, though. It's hard, it requires you to really understand what you studied, and it's a real challenge. I liked that a lot.

Then there was the rhythm. Every day I got **two flags**. Not three, no matter how much I wanted it.

Some days I'd have both flags by the afternoon and think, _"ok, maybe I can get one more today."_ Nope. Night would come, and I'd need to rest. Other days it was late at night, I was close to calling it, and the second flag of the day would finally show up at the very end. That surprised me every time: the same pace, day after day.

The good news is that you get **7 days**, so the timing is well designed. I finished in 5, with time left for the report.

## What was the hardest part?

Finding some of the vulnerabilities. Some of them you can spot just by reading the source code or playing around with parameters. Others don't show themselves at all, and if you don't think of testing for that specific vulnerability, you won't find it. That meant several hours of trying and trying until something finally worked.

## The report

I didn't expect to enjoy this part as much as I did. Taking everything you found and turning it into a professional report made the whole thing feel less like an exam and more like a real pentest engagement.

## What I'd recommend

- **Do at least 60% of the [CWES Preparation track](https://app.hackthebox.com/tracks/87).** The machines are very good. The week before the exam, I did one machine per day, then I took the exam.
- **Learn SysReptor before the exam.** It was new to me, and understanding how it works and how to write findings in it saved me a lot of time.
- **Write every finding in your notes app as you go.** I used Obsidian. Every two flags, I stopped and turned those raw notes into proper, professional text in SysReptor. That way the report doesn't pile up at the end, because it can be a lot.
- **Don't spend more than 6 hours on a single problem.** Pivot, move forward, and come back later with fresh eyes. It works more often than you'd think.
- **Take it easy.** Enjoy every machine in the exam and lean on your notes. They're there for a reason.
